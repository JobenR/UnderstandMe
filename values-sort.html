Values Card Sort

Find your top 10 values

A guided exercise to help you identify, narrow, and rank order the values that matter most to you right now.

What It Is

A tool for naming what matters, on purpose

A values card sort has a client sort words representing personal values — connection, autonomy, growth, and similar — into tiers of importance, narrowing down to a core handful.

What It Does

  • Clarifies values that are often felt but rarely named
  • Forces genuine prioritization, not just a vague list of "things that matter"
  • Concretizes abstract concepts into shared language you and the client can reference throughout treatment

How It's Used, Ongoing

Once identified, the values function as a compass, not a one-time intake exercise:

  • Away from values — avoidance, safety behaviors, or symptom-driven choices framed against a specific sacrificed value
  • Toward values — reframes hard or exposure-adjacent actions as evidence of value-consistent living, not just symptom reduction (especially useful in ERP/exposure work, where short-term discomfort needs meaning-based reinforcement, not relief)
  • Resolves ambivalence at stuck points ("is this toward values, or away from values?")
  • Supports recurring check-ins across sessions without re-explaining the concept
  • Keeps treatment anchored in ongoing direction, rather than collapsing once a specific goal is met

How It's Completed

Four simple steps

  1. Sort each card. You'll see {{TOTAL}} values, one at a time, each with a short definition. For each one, choose Most Important, Important, or Not Important — based on how you want to be or act, if you were able.
  2. Narrow down. Once every card is sorted, you'll narrow your strongest values down to a core group of 10.
  3. Rank order. Put those 10 in order, from most important (1) to least (10).
  4. Check alignment. For each of your top 10, rate how consistently you're actually living it right now.
  5. Get your results. You'll receive your rank-ordered values list, a life-domain balance snapshot, a reflection exercise for each value, and your full "Not Important" list for reference — moving toward one of those can be a sign of misalignment too.

There's no time limit, and you can take breaks between cards — your progress is saved in this browser.

Worth A Second Look

What "Priority Gap" means

A Priority Gap appears next to any of your top 3 values where you rated how consistently you're living it at 5 or below. It flags a value you say matters most, but aren't yet living out day to day — often the most useful place to focus, in session or on your own.
